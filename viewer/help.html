The Slipr concept

Slipr presentations bridge the gap between live keynote presentations and detailed written documentation. Traditional slide decks force an impossible compromise: either slides are dense walls of text unsuitable for a live audience, or they are brief visuals that become useless handouts afterwards.

Slipr solves this by storing one show file (show.json) that powers two distinct delivery experiences:

Audience mode
A polished, linear presentation for a room and big screen. High-level, punchy slides with step-by-step builds, smooth auto-animate transitions, dual-screen presenter view, and phone remote control.
Reader mode
An interactive, self-paced reading experience for individuals. Contains the slide visuals alongside a rich reader layer: deep explanations, code tabs, interactive diagrams, image hotspots, web embeds, and PDF documents, tailored to the reader's role and preferred detail level.
Key advantage: You author and maintain a single deck. When presenting live to stakeholders, use Audience mode. When sharing notes, follow-up briefings, or onboarding materials, share Reader mode.

Audience mode

Audience mode is built on Reveal.js and designed for confident, professional delivery on stage, in meeting rooms, or over video calls.

Presenter controls

You can advance slides using standard keyboard keys, presentation clickers, or touch gestures:

  • Next step or slide: →, Space, or PageDown.
  • Previous step or slide: ← or PageUp.
  • Fullscreen: Press F to enter or exit fullscreen.
  • Blackout screen: Press B or . to blank the projection when taking open questions or directing attention away from the screen. Press again to restore.

Fragments and auto-animate

Slides can reveal content incrementally using fragments (step-by-step appearance of cards, bullet points, or overlay images). When multiple consecutive slides share common layout elements, Slipr's auto-animate morphs titles, images, and card boxes smoothly between slides. A picture that appears on both slides moves, resizes and zooms (its crop changes) without being stretched. If your system is set to reduce motion, slides change without these animations.

Agenda slides & chapter bar

Shows structured into chapters automatically generate clean agenda slides introducing each section. A discreet progress bar along the bottom of the screen indicates chapter progress throughout the presentation.

Dual-screen presenter console

Press S to open the built-in speaker view in a separate window. Drag this window onto your private laptop display while projecting the main window onto the auditorium display. The speaker view includes:

  • Current slide display and upcoming slide preview.
  • Live speaker notes formatted in readable type.
  • Elapsed presentation timer and current wall clock.

Browser note: Your browser may prompt you to allow popups on localhost when pressing S for the first time.

Overview grid and zoom

  • Overview grid: Press O or Esc to see all slides as a grid of thumbnails that fits the screen, grouped by chapter. The row under the mouse grows so you can read it; the current slide has a coloured frame. Click a slide (or move with the arrow keys and press Enter) to go there; Esc or O takes you back to where you were. Backup slides come last, with a dashed border.
  • Area zoom: Hold Alt and click anywhere on a slide to zoom smoothly into that specific element or chart. Click again to zoom back out.

Laser pointer, spotlight and pen

Point at what you are talking about, or draw on the slide. One tool is on at a time; press its key again, or Esc, to turn it off.

  • Laser pointer: press L. A red dot follows the mouse (the arrow is hidden).
  • Spotlight: press K. The slide darkens except for a circle around the mouse.
  • Pen: press D and draw with the mouse, a pen or your finger (drawing never swipes to another slide). X erases this slide's drawing. Each slide keeps its own drawing while you present, so going back shows it again; nothing is saved, and reloading the page starts clean.

In Slipr, L and K are these tools, not Reveal.js's alternative keys for next slide and up. Use the arrow keys, Space or your clicker to move on.

Continuing in Reader mode

Press R at any point during a talk to seamlessly switch the viewer into Reader mode at the current slide, allowing you or your audience to inspect deep-dive background material.

Phone remote control (secondary device)

Slipr includes a built-in mobile remote control that runs directly in your phone's web browser. You do not need to install any mobile app from an app store. Your phone pairs instantly with the laptop presentation over a local WebSocket connection, giving you tactile navigation buttons, live speaker notes, and an elapsed presentation timer in the palm of your hand.

Starting the relay server

Open a terminal on your laptop in the Slipr project folder and start the local relay server:

npm run remote

The relay starts on port 8080 (or your custom port) and automatically detects your laptop's local area network (LAN) IP address.

Pairing your phone

  1. Open your presentation in Audience mode.
  2. Press P on your keyboard or click the 📱 button in the bottom-right corner of the slide.
  3. A pairing modal appears displaying a standalone SVG QR code containing your session address and an authentication token.
  4. Open your phone's camera app or QR scanner and scan the QR code. Your phone opens http://<laptop-ip>:8080/viewer/remote/?session=....
  5. The remote connects immediately and the modal on your laptop displays "Phone connected".

Using your phone's personal hotspot

When presenting at venues without accessible Wi-Fi, or where corporate guest networks isolate connected devices (preventing devices from communicating with each other), turn on your phone's Personal Hotspot and connect your laptop's Wi-Fi to your phone.

Troubleshooting: "Cannot reach page" on personal hotspot

If your laptop is connected to your phone's personal hotspot but opening the remote URL or scanning the QR code results in a browser error such as "Cannot reach page", "Server took too long to respond", or a connection timeout, the issue is almost always Windows Defender Firewall blocking incoming connections.

Why this happens: When a Windows laptop connects to a mobile personal hotspot (such as an iPhone or Android hotspot on 172.20.10.x or 192.168.43.x), Windows classifies the new network adapter profile as a Public network for security. On Public networks, Windows Firewall blocks all inbound connections by default, preventing your phone from reaching port 8080 on your laptop.

Use either of the following two solutions to resolve this:

Solution 1: Switch the Wi-Fi connection to "Private" in Windows (Recommended)

Switching your phone hotspot's network profile from Public to Private tells Windows that the network is trusted, allowing local connections between your phone and laptop:

  • Option A — Via Windows Settings:
    1. Press Win + I to open Windows Settings.
    2. Go to Network & internet → Wi-Fi.
    3. Click on the active Wi-Fi connection (your phone's hotspot name).
    4. Under Network profile type, select Private network.
  • Option B — Via PowerShell (Administrator): 
    # Check current connection profile
Get-NetConnectionProfile

# Switch Wi-Fi interface to Private
Set-NetConnectionProfile -InterfaceAlias "Wi-Fi" -NetworkCategory Private

Solution 2: Allow Node.js through Windows Defender Firewall

If corporate policies require keeping network profiles set to Public, explicitly allow Node.js inbound traffic on Public networks:

  1. Press the Windows key, type "Allow an app through Windows Firewall", and press Enter.
  2. Click the Change settings button (requires administrator rights).
  3. Scroll through the list of allowed apps to find Node.js: Server-side JavaScript.
  4. Ensure that the checkbox for Public is checked (in addition to Private).
  5. Click OK to apply.

Once adjusted, refresh the remote page on your phone or re-scan the QR code, and the remote UI will connect immediately.

Reader mode

Reader mode turns your presentation into an engaging, interactive document. Built from the ground up without Reveal.js, it offers clean responsive typography, chapter navigation, progress tracking, and personalized detail levels.

Screen layout

  • Top bar: Contains the show title, chapter drawer toggle (on mobile), the ⚙ Tailor panel, ⌕ Search, ✎ My notes, and a Present ▶ button to switch to Audience mode.
  • Table of contents drawer: On wide desktop displays, the table of contents sits in a left navigation pane. On narrow screens and mobile phones, it opens smoothly from the menu icon. Shows read progress ("n of m read") and visited checkmarks.
  • Article body: Each slide is presented as a section with its visual slide canvas at the top, followed by speaker notes and the rich reader layer blocks.
  • Keyboard navigation: Press J to jump to the next slide on your path, and K to jump to the previous slide.

Returning to Audience mode

Click Present ▶ in the top bar or press A on your keyboard to launch Audience mode directly at the slide you are currently reading.

Tailoring & profiles

Not every reader needs the same information. A developer wants architecture diagrams and code samples; an executive needs executive summaries and strategic trade-offs. Reader mode lets users tailor what they see without altering the show file.

The Tailor panel (⚙)

Click the ⚙ Tailor button in the top bar or press D to open the tailoring panel:

Audience profiles
Select a role (e.g. Developer, Manager, Architect). Blocks targeted at other roles are hidden, while blocks designated for all profiles remain visible.
Detail levels (Depth 1 to 3)
  • Level 1 (Essentials): High-level overview; only primary summaries and foundational blocks are shown.
  • Level 2 (Standard): The default reading experience with standard explanations.
  • Level 3 (Deep dive): Shows every block, including extensive technical appendices, deep code tabs, and extended background.

You can press D anytime while reading to cycle through detail levels instantly.

Reading routes (Paths)
Select a curated route through the show (e.g. 5-minute overview or Full architectural walkthrough). The table of contents and J/K keys guide you through only the slides included on that path.
Presenter notes
Toggle whether speaker notes appear under each slide as "What the presenter said" collapsible sections.

Interactive blocks

The reader layer supports a variety of rich, interactive content blocks that bring depth to your slides:

Cards

Structured, responsive grids of cards with icons, subtitles, and bullet points. Perfect for feature matrices, market overviews, or team rosters.

Tabs

Multi-tabbed interactive containers allowing readers to switch between alternative code implementations, multiple languages, or before/after comparisons without cluttering the page.

Image hotspots

Interactive numbered pins placed over diagrams or photographs. Clicking or hovering a pin opens a popover detailing what that part of the image represents.

Embedded web pages

Sandboxed iframes for live interactive web apps, dashboards, or video demonstrations. To protect privacy and adhere to offline-first principles, external sites show an explicit "Load embed" button before establishing connections, with link-card fallbacks and an "Open in new tab" button.

PDF documents

Embedded PDF documents rendered via vendored PDF.js. Readers can page through documents, zoom in and out, copy text, and search across PDF page contents directly from Slipr's search index.

Graphs

Networks of ideas, parts or people, drawn with vendored Cytoscape.js. Drag to move around, use +, − and Fit to zoom, and click or tap a node (or choose it in the Node… list) to highlight its links and read about it; Go to slide jumps to its slide. The coloured tag buttons show what the colours mean and hide or show those nodes. Esc clears the selection. While presenting, a graph can grow click by click.

Mermaid diagrams

Flowcharts, sequence diagrams, state machines, and class diagrams rendered dynamically as clean SVGs using vendored Mermaid. Diagrams scale smoothly, adapt to light/dark themes, and render entirely offline without external network requests.

Glossary & popups

Slipr makes domain knowledge accessible without interrupting reading flow:

  • Glossary terms: Terms defined in the show glossary are automatically highlighted with a subtle dotted underline on slides and reader blocks (or explicitly authored via [[term]]). Hovering or clicking a term displays an instant popover with its definition, aliases, and a link to view all glossary entries.
  • Popups: Author footnotes and parenthetical clarifications using ^[popup text] syntax. Readers can hover or tap to read the note in place.

Personal annotations & notes

Slipr provides comprehensive annotation tools stored privately in your browser's local storage or IndexedDB:

Highlights and text notes

Select any text passage on a slide or reader block with your mouse or finger. A tooltip appears allowing you to Highlight the text or Add note. Highlights persist across browser sessions and anchor robustly even if surrounding text changes.

Slide notes and object pins

  • Slide notes: Click ✎ Note next to a slide header to write private markdown notes for that entire slide.
  • Object pins: Click 📍 Pin on a slide to turn it into a pin board. Click anywhere on a slide element (a specific bullet, image, or card) to attach a numbered pin note. Hovering a pin reveals its note.

Bookmarks

Click the ★ bookmark icon on any slide or press M to bookmark it. Bookmarked slides are pinned to the top of the table of contents for quick reference.

"My notes" panel (N)

Click the ✎ My notes button in the top bar or press N to open your personal annotation notebook. All highlights, slide notes, pins, and bookmarks are gathered here, grouped by slide. Clicking any note scrolls directly to that item in the article.

Exporting and importing annotations

Inside the "My notes" panel:

  • Export Markdown: Generates a clean, readable .md document of all your highlights and notes, ready to paste into Notion, Obsidian, or share with colleagues.
  • Export JSON: Backs up your raw annotations for safe keeping.
  • Import JSON: Restores annotations or transfers them to another browser or device. Slipr merges notes intelligently, preserving the latest edit.

Full-text search

Press / or Ctrl + K anywhere in Reader mode (or click the ⌕ Search button in the top bar) to open the instant search modal.

  • Comprehensive index: Searches across slide titles, slide body copy, reader blocks, tabs, cards, image alt texts, glossary terms and aliases, and embedded PDF documents.
  • Instant & fuzzy: Matches word beginnings (e.g. typing "arch" finds "architecture") and ignores accents and letter casing.
  • On-page highlighting: Selecting a result jumps directly to the target block and highlights the matching words on screen using the CSS Custom Highlight API.
  • Tailoring awareness: If matching content is hidden by your current profile or depth setting, the search dialog reports the count of hidden matches with a one-click "show everything" option.

Opening & sharing shows

Slipr provides flexible options for opening and distributing presentations:

URL parameters

Point your browser to the viewer with query parameters:

# Open in Audience mode
http://localhost:8080/viewer/?show=../shows/demo/show.json

# Open in Reader mode
http://localhost:8080/viewer/?show=../shows/demo/show.json&mode=reader

# Pre-tailored link for a specific role and depth
http://localhost:8080/viewer/?show=../shows/demo/show.json&mode=reader&profile=dev&depth=3&path=quick

Local show folders (File System Access API)

On Chrome and Edge, open viewer/ without parameters and click Open a show folder… to pick a show folder from your disk. Slipr loads the show directly without uploading your files to any external server. The folder is remembered in your browser under viewer/?folder=<key>.

Zipped presentations (?zip=<url>)

Load and present a zipped Slipr presentation directly from any web server or GitHub release via a URL parameter:

# Load and present from a zip archive
http://localhost:8080/viewer/?zip=https://example.com/show.zip

# Open directly in Reader mode
http://localhost:8080/viewer/?zip=https://example.com/show.zip&mode=reader

# GitHub archive or multi-show repository
http://localhost:8080/viewer/?zip=https://example.com/repo-main.zip&path=shows/intro

The viewer downloads the zip file client-side, showing download progress, and decompresses it entirely in memory using standard browser streams (keeping Slipr fully offline once loaded, with zero backend servers needed). Images and assets become in-memory blob URLs.

GitHub compatibility: Zip files from GitHub (where files are enclosed in a top-level folder such as repo-main/show.json) are auto-detected automatically. For repositories with multiple presentations, specify which one to present with the &path= parameter.

Importing local zip archives (.zip / .slipr)

You can open a local .zip or .slipr presentation archive from your computer directly in the viewer without running a server or uploading any files:

  • File chooser: Click Open zip archive… on the viewer start page to choose a presentation archive from your disk. Works in all modern browsers (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari).
  • Drag and drop: Drag and drop any .zip or .slipr file directly onto the viewer start page.

The archive is decompressed in memory using browser Web Streams. The presentation is remembered in browser storage under viewer/?archive=<key>, enabling seamless mode switching (A / R) between Audience and Reader mode, page reloads, and deep links. If the archive contains multiple shows, a choice list lets you choose which one to present.

Single-file HTML exports (offline presenting)

Shows can be compiled into a single, self-contained offline HTML file using the Slipr Editor's Export… button or the CLI:

npm run export -- shows/demo

The resulting exports/demo.html contains the entire presentation, template styles, fonts, images, diagrams, and runtime bundled together. It requires zero backend servers, zero installations, and presents seamlessly offline when double-clicked on any computer.

Troubleshooting

Dev server or relay not responding

Slipr requires a local HTTP server for loading shows and WebSocket connections:

  • Start the static file server: npm run serve (serves on http://localhost:8080).
  • Start the mobile remote relay server: npm run remote (serves static files and WebSocket relay on port 8080).

Phone remote "Can't reach page" / Connection timed out

If you are connected via your phone's personal hotspot and the phone cannot load the remote URL:

  1. Ensure npm run remote is actively running in your terminal.
  2. Check your Windows network profile. When connecting to a phone hotspot, Windows defaults to a Public network, causing Windows Defender Firewall to block inbound traffic.
  3. Fix: Switch your Wi-Fi profile from Public to Private in Windows Settings (Win + I → Network & internet → Wi-Fi → click hotspot name → select Private network) or run Set-NetConnectionProfile -InterfaceAlias "Wi-Fi" -NetworkCategory Private in PowerShell.
  4. Alternatively, allow Node.js: Server-side JavaScript through Windows Defender Firewall for Public networks. See the Phone remote control section for step-by-step instructions.

Guest Wi-Fi client isolation

Some corporate, hotel, or conference Wi-Fi networks enable Client Isolation (preventing devices on the same Wi-Fi network from communicating with one another). When client isolation is enabled, your phone cannot connect to your laptop even if both are on the same Wi-Fi. In this scenario, switch to using your phone's Personal Hotspot as documented above.

Folder permission lost after browser restart

When opening a show via a local folder handle (?folder=<key>), the browser may require you to re-confirm permission after restarting Chrome or Edge. Simply click the Allow access button on the prompt page to resume.

Validation errors on show.json

If a show fails to open, the viewer displays a structured diagnostic report detailing schema violations, missing assets, or invalid layout references. Open the show in the Slipr Editor (editor/) to visually inspect and correct invalid slots or missing files without editing raw JSON.

Zip download failure or CORS restrictions

When loading a presentation from a remote zip URL (?zip=<url>), browsers block cross-origin HTTP requests unless the server responds with Access-Control-Allow-Origin headers (CORS).

  • GitHub: Raw archive links from GitHub may restrict cross-origin requests. Host the zip file on GitHub Pages or a CORS-enabled static file host.
  • Custom servers: Ensure your web server includes the Access-Control-Allow-Origin: * header for zip assets.
  • Local fallback: If you cannot enable CORS on the remote host, download the zip file to your computer, extract it, and click Open a show folder… on the viewer start page to present completely offline without restrictions.
